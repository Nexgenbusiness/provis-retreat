5 / 11
Method

No specialty roaster discloses revenue.
Not one.

Across roughly forty primary sources — Daily Coffee News, Sprudge, Fresh Cup, Upstate Business Journal, Crain's, BevNET, PRNewswire and every reachable founder interview — there is not one dollar figure for Black & White, Madcap or Methodical.

The only disclosed financial ratio in the entire peer set is Methodical's "wholesale is approximately 50% of revenue."

Every revenue number in circulation for these companies is modelled — including ours. The data brokers are worse than useless: Buzzfile put Black & White at $2.0M against a published volume implying $10–12M, and Compworth put FairWave at $16.9M against its own headcount band of 501–1,000.

The real benchmark currency is roasting volume and facility square footage, which companies do publish.

The pattern

Operators who have run this experiment
are moving away from it

Peet's — Capital Markets Day 2025

"FROM: Company-owned stores with regional footprint → TO: National footprint with franchise partners."

Proud Mary — SEC use of proceeds

Expanding into "smaller to-go models… repeatable, scaleable designs, build, equipment, service and technology."

Both reached scale on company-owned cafés with food programs. Both are now deliberately reducing that exposure.

Two of three roaster peers also shrank. Black & White closed Rolesville — its only location with a real kitchen. Madcap sold Leland. Only Methodical is still opening, at a stated "at least one cafe a year."

8 / 11
Food

The one peer who bought their bakery

Methodical acquired Bake Room outright in March 2024 rather than outsourcing it. The reported result: "pastry sales exploded in 2024."

They are the only peer in the set with a genuine kitchen program, the largest headcount band of the three, the largest roastery at 39,500 sq ft, and the only one still expanding.

Every other peer outsources pastry — Madcap to Give Thanks Bakery and Bev's Bagels, Black & White to Union Special, Elixr to local bakeries across all eight of its cafés.

Elixr runs eight cafés with zero kitchens and a 12kg Diedrich — one-sixth of Onyx's roasting capacity. A café company that happens to roast.

What this changes

$600K is the honest base case.
$1M requires a structural asset.

$600KIndependent sit-down café
$595KReborn, audited, 10 cafés
$434KMedian café for sale
$976KBranded urban inline

The $600K base is corroborated four independent ways — SEC-filed independents clustering at $578–596K, Reborn's audited $595K per café across ten, Biggby inline at $582K, and a $434K median across 51 cafés listed for sale.

Clearing $1M is not a matter ofexecution or optimism. The data names the assets precisely: a drive-thru (+25–32%, replicated across four brands), a real food program (Panera $2.54M at 19.3% store EBITDA), captive traffic, or an established brand in a dense urban market.

The cautionary case is Bad Ass Coffee — a sit-down café with a full food program, and a $545K median. The food program alone did not carry it, because the brand did not command a premium.

Our 175 customers/day at $8.00 produces $504K, and 250 at $8.50 produces $765K. Both sit inside the defensible band — the model lands correctly.

What it means for us

The plan is better calibrated
than the first read suggested

3–5 cafés is right. Peer median is 3.5, and two peers are shrinking rather than building.

Wholesale at 3,000 lb/week is realistic — roughly 100–150 accounts, a decade of steady selling.

Bootstrapped is normal. Eight of ten peers took no institutional capital.

Our wholesale is underpriced. $12.25 Founding tier against a peer band of $16–19. That sits under 43% of our future poundage and costs nothing to fix on the next contract.

The subscription thesis still has no precedent. No peer reached $10M on a DTC book. That finding survived every correction.
