The central question

Has anyone reached $10M
with three cafés or fewer?

Yes. Two of them. Neither did it with subscriptions.

Proud Mary US · $11.5M on 2 cafés

The cafés are restaurants. Full kitchens, seven days, Australian all-day brunch. Revenue per café is $3.7M — five times the peer median.

The caveats are severe: two consecutive loss years at $13–14M of revenue, a 32% gross margin, and a profit that is 58% one-off government credits. They also took outside money and hired Stumptown's former EVP to run the US business.

Black & White · $8.5M on 3 cafés

The company is a factory. 520,000 lb a year through a 14,000 sq ft plant with three Loring roasters. Half wholesale, a quarter DTC on two to three new releases every week.

Cafés are 25% of revenue. The founders were two US Barista Champions — not a replicable input. Sold to private equity at eight years old.

The pattern: to reach $10M on three cafés or fewer you either make the cafés restaurants, or make the company a wholesale factory. There is no third example.

The finding that matters most

No one has done it on subscriptions

40%Highest DTC share — Passenger
$6.5MPassenger's revenue
57%Our plan's DTC share

DTC share has no relationship to company size. Sey is 37% DTC at $3.1M. Elixr is 5% DTC at $9.0M. The two largest companies sit at 30% and 13%.

The closest anyone comes to a subscription-led business is Passenger at 40% DTC and $6.5M — and that DTC rests on a frozen-green sourcing moat letting them sell single origins year-round, not on a subscriber count.

Our plan routes 57% of revenue through a 10,000-subscriber book. That is a higher share than any peer, at a larger size than the highest-share peer achieves.

5 / 9
Our plan

What the evidence supports

3–5 cafés is exactly right. Peer median is 3.5, and café count is the second-strongest predictor of revenue in the set.

3,000 lb a week of wholesale is realistic. Methodical does ~290,000 lb on 200 accounts; Black & White ~354,000 lb. We would need roughly 100–150 accounts — a decade of steady selling, not a miracle.

Bootstrapped is normal here. Nine of ten peers took no institutional capital. Onyx explicitly disclaims investors.

The infrastructure is unremarkable. At $10M: one 35kg roaster, ~12,000 sq ft, ~$420K of green inventory. Capacity will not be the constraint. Demand will.

6 / 9
Our plan

What the evidence does not support

1 · The 10,000-subscriber target carries 57% of revenue and requires a 63–96× increase in monthly subscriber acquisition, sustained for a decade. Should be cut to 2,000–4,000 and re-based.

2 · "22% annual growth from year 3" conceals ~391% a year in years one and two. The café is the step change. Say so plainly.

3 · $12.25/lb wholesale is the bottom of the peer band. It makes 43% of the pounds carry 17% of the revenue.

4 · The plan assumes three cafés' worth of brand reach on 5,137 followers — in a category where audience is the strongest single predictor of revenue.

5 · The plan assumes margin improves with scale. The only audited peer says it does not.

The verdict

Achievable, but top-decile —
and not by this route

Two of ten peers reached roughly $10M within a decade. Onyx at year ten, Black & White at year eight before selling. It has been done.

But the median peer sits at $7–8M after twelve to fourteen years, and both fast movers had non-replicable assets — multiple national champions, record auction wins, a full-time creator on staff.

The café count, the wholesale poundage and the infrastructure plan are all well-calibrated. The problem is that the plan routes 57% of its revenue through a subscription book no comparable company has ever built.

Re-base it onto the two routes peers have actually used: cafés that sell food after 11am, and wholesale priced above $12.25.
